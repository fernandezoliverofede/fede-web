Culture & Change

Culture change that actually sticks — by working on the behaviors, management practices, and organizational systems that shape how people work every day.

Why culture change initiatives often fall short

Culture is one of those organizational topics where intentions and outcomes tend to diverge most significantly. Organizations articulate new values, launch culture initiatives, and invest in engagement programs — and yet the culture remains stubbornly resistant to change.

The reason is usually that culture is treated as something separate from how the organization actually works. But culture isn't defined by what organizations say they value. It's defined by what they actually reward, tolerate, and reinforce through everyday management practices, decisions, and systems.

What this work covers

  • Culture diagnosis — understanding the gap between espoused and actual culture
  • Identifying the structural, process, and behavioral drivers of current culture
  • Defining a clear, realistic picture of the culture needed to support business strategy
  • Designing the levers — management practices, rituals, reinforcement mechanisms — that can shift culture meaningfully
  • Supporting organizational change processes — mergers, restructuring, strategic pivots
  • Change leadership and change management — helping leaders navigate and lead change

Culture is a product of the system

If you want to change culture, you need to change the system that produces it. That means looking at how decisions get made, how performance gets evaluated, what behaviors get rewarded and what behaviors get tolerated, and how leaders model the working norms they say they want.

This work tends to be less about culture programs and more about organizational system design: making sure the everyday experience of working in the organization reinforces the culture you actually want.

Who this is for

Organizations going through significant change — strategic transformation, growth, mergers, post-acquisition integration — and organizations that have identified a gap between the culture they have and the culture they need to achieve their business ambitions.
